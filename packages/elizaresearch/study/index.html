Staying independent as we age takes a lot of invisible work. Families coordinate appointments, rides, services, and follow-ups. Even in a well-run community, someone has to connect the dots.

51M

Americans care for someone 50+, providing an average of 26 hours of unpaid support per week.

Our aim

Help older adults stay independent for longer, which is closely tied to control, wellbeing, and quality of life.

The question behind the study

Where do seniors rely on others to manage everyday life — and what could help them stay in control while easing the load on families and staff?

Who we are

We're a small team of technologists conducting this study with the Own Your Data Foundation, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit focused on helping people use technology safely and with greater control over their data and digital lives.

Common questions

For seniors and family caregivers

Is this a product pilot or sales pitch?

No. This is user research on technology use and independence. You're not being asked to purchase, pilot, or commit to anything.

Is this medical research or medical advice?

No. We study technology use, coordination, and independence. We don't provide medical care or advice, and we never ask for medical records.

What information are you collecting?

Your experiences coordinating everyday life and using technology. Findings go into anonymized cross-community research; your name is never published.

Will I have to share sensitive information?

You may describe tasks you find difficult, and some sessions include watching how you use your devices to complete tasks. You control what you show and can skip anything.

For communities

How much work is required from our staff?

Mostly helping us identify appropriate residents, family members, and staff. Wherever possible we handle scheduling and coordination directly.

What will you use this information for?

To understand where better tools could support resident independence and reduce coordination work. Findings go into your community's report and, in anonymized form, into cross-community research.
